How a question is answered

  1. Statutory concordance. A section cited with its Act (e.g. "Section 103 BNS") is expanded with its old/new-code equivalent ("Section 302 IPC"), so queries in BNS terms still reach IPC-era judgments.
  2. Filters (optional). Year range and minimum bench strength are resolved in SQLite to judgment IDs, which restrict both searches.
  3. Two searches. Dense search (bge-small-en-v1.5, cosine, ChromaDB) and BM25 keyword search each return their top 60 passages. BM25 catches exact statutory tokens that embeddings blur.
  4. Reciprocal Rank Fusion merges the two rankings by rank only.
  5. Cross-encoder rerank. bge-reranker-base reads question and passage together. A bounded authority prior (at most ±5%) prefers larger benches only between near-equally relevant passages.
  6. One result per judgment. Passages are grouped by judgment; a judgment scores as its best passage and keeps up to two passages.
  7. Grounded answer. The LLM sees one [Precedent N] per judgment with its metadata and passages, must cite only those, and must refuse when they do not answer. If the best relevance is below the refusal threshold, no LLM call is made. A citation check (no LLM) flags any citation that was not provided.

Data

All criminal judgments (by case number) of the Supreme Court of India, 2018–2025, reported in the S.C.R., from the AWS Open Data registry "indian-supreme-court-judgments". Official metadata, PDF text cleaned of page furniture, headnotes separated from judgment text.

Engineering

CPU-only, small pretrained models (nothing is trained), a process-tree RAM guard, resumable per-collection indexing, deterministic chunk IDs, and an LLM provider chain with rate limiting and a response cache.
